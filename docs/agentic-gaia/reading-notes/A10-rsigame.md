## What the paper is
A game-improvement loop on top of an already generated game P0 (GameCraft-Bench tasks, instantiated in both Godot and Phaser; Sec. 4.1). **Local loop per round** (Sec. 3.1.1, App. C.4): Controller (planner; decides one development question and the cheapest way to answer it: reuse existing evidence, explore, or escalate), Explorer (a play agent that boots a scenario and plays; Qwen3.8-27B), Editor (edits the project, up to 26 tool calls per round), Verifier (replays every shipped demo and answers `goal_achieved` yes/no/unclear and a `broken_now` list), plus an evolving **checklist** that accumulates issues and verification results. **Global loop** (Sec. 3.1.2, App. C.2): every 3 rounds a Global Quality Monitor replays the retained "champion" and the candidate on the same demos, a comparator reads paired frames (told neither which build is newer nor that anything was improved), and the candidate is adopted only if the stage-brief half of a proxy rubric improves by at least 0.05 without the task half falling by more than 0.05; development stops after K = 3 checkpoints without a new champion (saturation), optionally continuing with a one-off "stage brief" written by a human or a stronger model. **Training-based part** (Sec. 3.2, App. E): supervised LoRA fine-tuning of Qwen3.8-27B on GPT-5.5 generation traces, distilled plans and verified improvement rounds from GLM-5.3-Flash runs.

## Key results (with locators)
1. **Main table (Sec. 4.2, Table 1, p. 6).** Overall scores on 140 tasks (Godot): Codex + GPT-5.5 base 50.26, + Play2Code 50.74, + RSIGame 64.53; Kimi-K2.6 29.63 / 35.19 / 44.77; GLM-5.3-Flash 29.46 / 38.59 / 49.72; Qwen3.8-27B 37.07 / 40.53 / 47.77; Qwen3.8-27B (SFT) 48.22 / 49.71 / 61.38. Phaser: OpenGame + GPT-5.5 49.44 / 55.10 / 58.21; Qwen3.8-27B 40.03 / 48.70 / 50.24; Qwen3.8-27B (SFT) 45.14 / 53.15 / 58.53. RSIGame gains over base: +10.7 to +20.3 (Godot), +8.8 to +13.4 (Phaser); over Play2Code: +7.2 to +13.8 (Godot) and +1.5 to +5.4 (Phaser; computed from Table 1). Billable tokens are within about 10% between RSIGame and Play2Code in most rows (e.g. GPT-5.5: 3.12M vs 3.20M; Qwen: 9.13M vs 9.92M), the exception being GLM-5.3-Flash (5.06M vs 3.18M). RSIGame development cost per task is about $0.88-1.25 on Godot and $1.44-1.53 on Phaser.
2. **Budget and judge (Sec. 4.1, App. D.1-D.3).** Both methods get up to 30 rounds and 26 tool calls per round. Every score is the mean of three independent replay-and-score runs by a Qwen3.8-27B judge (T=0; 16 uniform frames per demo; 12-21 rubric items per task), not the GPT-5.5 judge of GameCraft-Bench; scores are therefore not comparable to A2's table (e.g. GPT-5.5 one-shot is 50.26 here vs 39.49 in A2). Replay noise: across 529 artifacts scored three times, the median range between runs is 0.73 points (p75 3.33); 225 are identical in all three; the families with the highest median range are sports 4.66, horror 3.37, openworld 1.41 and the lowest strategy/rhythm/idle/racing 0.00 (Tables 10-11).
3. **Statistics (App. D.5, Table 14).** Paired task bootstrap and Wilcoxon: RSIGame - Base +14.26 (GPT-5.5, Godot), +10.70 (Qwen, Godot), +8.77 and +10.21 (Phaser); RSIGame - Play2Code +13.79 and +7.24 (Godot), +3.11 (GPT-5.5, Phaser, p = 0.008) but +1.55 [-1.50, 4.66] p = 0.394 for Qwen on Phaser (not significant).
4. **Cross-judge check (App. D.3, Table 12).** On 40 family-stratified Godot tasks, re-scoring the same replays with GPT-5.5 instead of Qwen3.8-27B preserves the ordering RSIGame > Play2Code > Base (gain over base +14.04 vs +12.04), agreement on direction 37/40; absolute scores only moderately correlated (rho = 0.64). Play2Code - Base is +0.56 [-3.45, +4.21] under the Qwen judge and +0.28 [-3.83, +3.83] under the GPT-5.5 judge.
5. **Does naive iteration overfit? (Sec. 5, Fig. 3, App. D.4, Table 13).** Development-time scaling on 40 tasks: "Play2Code quickly plateaus and often regresses as more rounds are added"; RSIGame converts extra rounds into gains; the Global Quality Monitor "retains the best state reached so far" and removes volatility (Fig. 3; Fig. 4a: retained checkpoint tracks the oracle-best checkpoint). Free-play check (20 family-stratified Godot tasks, blind play agents with 40 actions and one reset, scripted replays never shown): RSIGame preferred over base on 17/20 (p = 0.003) and over Play2Code on 18/20 (p < 0.001); Play2Code vs base 9/11 (p = 0.824); agreement with the benchmark ordering 45/59 decided comparisons. This shows RSIGame's gains are not confined to the shipped replay scripts; it does not itself show that Play2Code's failure is caused by overfitting to the replay scripts.
6. **Saturation stop (App. C.2, Fig. 4b).** Checkpoints every 3 rounds ("the shortest interval that contains an improvement, its verification and a replay"); K = 1..4 compared against fixed budgets, "K = 3 sits at the knee"; a case study (Block Cascade, F.2.3) shows the loop correctly stopping at round 9 and delivering round 0 because nothing could be improved; another (Lawn Guardians, Fig. 10) stops at round 21 and delivers round 12, nine rounds early.
7. **Adaptive direction vs round-robin (Sec. 5, Fig. 5, App. D.6).** 16 paired games, 30 rounds each: blind pairwise preference (63 comparisons) 59% adaptive vs 37% round-robin, with adaptive spending more rounds on repair after a build break and on art when visuals lag.
8. **Verification audit (Sec. 5, Table 2, App. D.7).** Claude Opus 5 (judging each case twice, disagreements reviewed manually) audited trajectories from 40 GPT-5.5-initialised games: pre-edit targets grounded in evidence 72.3% for agentic verification vs 58.6% for a free-form critic (0.50 vs 1.93 ungrounded targets per round); post-edit failure detection over 48 adjudicated rounds: replay + verification failure recall 76.2%, specificity 92.6%, balanced accuracy 84.4%; build-only checking recall 0.0%, specificity 100%, balanced accuracy 50.0%.
9. **Experience internalisation (Sec. 3.2, 5, App. E, Table 18).** SFT corpus: 2,213 GPT-5.5 generation trajectories, 2,108 plans, 2,013 verified improvement rounds (of 4,003; GLM-5.3-Flash runs of the RSIGame loop), with the 140 benchmark task ids excluded (zero overlap re-scan). One-shot Godot overall for Qwen3.8-27B: base 37.07; + generation traces 41.45; + plans 41.77; + verified improvement rounds 48.22. The headline "Qwen reaches 61.38, exceeding GPT-5.5 one-shot" compares a fine-tuned (partly distilled from GPT-5.5 traces) model after the RSIGame loop with GPT-5.5's single pass (50.26); the matched comparison is GPT-5.5 + RSIGame 64.53.
10. **Another multi-agent system (App. F.1, Table 19).** VibeGame (eight roles all set to GPT-5.5, one trial per task, 40 Phaser tasks) scores 30.5 vs 46.0 for a one-shot reference; on 12 shared tasks 23.3 vs 50.1 one-shot vs 62.4 RSIGame. The authors read this as evidence that having verifier roles is not sufficient; the comparison is single-trial and "only to the extent of setting its role models".
11. **Human vs model "director" (App. C.3, D.8).** A stage-brief interface where a director plays the build with a budget of 60 actions and 2 resets (a key press costs one action however long held, a click costs one; watching, waiting and reading state are free); a model plays via a command-line client (open, press, hold, click, wait, look, reset, state, submit) that returns the frames captured after each action. Single example (Alley Brawlers): the human asked for art/onboarding, the model (Claude Opus 5, 25/60 actions) noticed the opponent never lost health and asked for working mechanics; branches reached 62.0 (human), 61.5 (model), 60.0 (no brief). Later stages are preferred in blind pairwise play (Fig. 9, wide intervals).
12. **Stated limitations (App. B).** Only four rubric dimensions (no originality, narrative, long-term engagement); compact games only; guidance mechanism evaluated on a limited set of cases.

## Corrections to the current literature-review.md entry (A10)
Entry: "naive iterative refinement overfits a small set of tests. A local explore-diagnose-improve loop plus a global loop that keeps the best checkpoint and detects regression improves quality under matched budgets on GameCraft-Bench tasks. Informs: checkpoint/best-so-far retention; overfitting to test cases." Depth: "abstract (WF)".
- "Naive iterative refinement overfits a small set of tests" is the authors' framing in the abstract and intro. The paper's evidence is that its Play2Code baseline plateaus or regresses with more rounds (Fig. 3; +0.56 [-3.45, +4.21] over base in the 40-task cross-judge subset) and that RSIGame's gains survive blind free play (17/20). It does not run a controlled test of overfitting to the replay scripts for the baseline. Treat "overfits" as the authors' hypothesis supported indirectly.
- "Matched budgets": matched on rounds (30) and per-round tool calls (26) and approximately on billable tokens; not matched for the training-based rows (SFT) and not for the headline "Qwen beats GPT-5.5 one-shot" claim.
- "On GameCraft-Bench tasks": the scores use the same tasks and rubric formula but a different judge (Qwen3.8-27B, 16 frames per demo, mean of 3 replays); its numbers cannot be compared with A2's table. Phaser is an additional engine (cross-engine check), not in A2.
- Missing: the judge model (Qwen3.8-27B) is also the play-agent (Explorer) model and one of the generators; a GPT-5.5 cross-judge check on 40 tasks agrees on ordering (rho = 0.64 on absolute scores).
- Missing: the "best checkpoint" mechanism is not "best by benchmark score": a separate Monitor with its own proxy rubric and paired-frame comparison chooses it, isolated from the benchmark rubric. This is the transferable design; reporting it as "keeps the best checkpoint" undersells the isolation requirement.
- Missing: the strongest design-relevant measurement is the verification audit (replay-based verification detects 76.2% of failed edits vs 0% for build-only), which is not mentioned.
- Missing: every method comparison is against one baseline (Play2Code), re-run by the authors under their own harness; no comparison to Play2Code's own published numbers.
- Venue: unreviewed arXiv preprint (v1), 47 pp., industry co-authorship (ByteDance); no venue claimed.

## Design relevance
- **Verify behaviour by replaying the same inputs before and after an edit.** The Verifier replays every shipped demo and asks whether the goal is visibly true and whether anything is plainly broken (input produces no change, something covers the playfield, screen frozen, unreadable text). Build-only checking caught 0 of the failed edits and replay-based verification 76.2% (n = 48). For Gaia: an edit tool should return a before/after comparison after advancing the same scripted time, not only "eval succeeded".
- **Frame-pair protocol for the contact-sheet tool.** The comparator gets each moment just before and just after an input for the old and new build, and two frames after each input, because replays are not frame-exact ("up to a frame or two apart"); it is told neither which build is newer nor that an improvement happened, and has an `unobserved` option so that not seeing something is not read as missing. Gaia's contact sheet should be anchored on input events, include at least two frames after each, and be paired against a baseline snapshot.
- **Keep the best, and decide with a different instrument than the one that edits.** Champion/candidate selection with a 0.05 margin, a floor on previously met requirements, checkpoints every 3 rounds and K = 3 patience. Only the comparator reads pixels; the decision layer reads structured counts. This is a concrete way to use screenshots plus structured state together and to prevent a model from grading its own expectation.
- **Budgets that worked.** 26 editor tool calls per round, 3 improvement attempts per round, 3 verification samples per claimed fix, 8 probe steps before editing, 16 frames per comparison, 30 rounds maximum, 1,800 s per round (Table 7); a human-or-model play session of 60 actions where observation (look, wait, state) is free and input is charged, which prevents the budget buying patience instead of interaction.
- **Do not let the agent grade only on its own demos.** Replay scripts are visible to the development agents; the authors added a free-play check with blind agents. For Gaia evaluation, hold out interaction scripts the editing agent never sees.
- **Role separation and withheld context.** Each prompt is told only what it needs (the planner receives the direction as a conclusion, the editor is told the problem is established and must not re-diagnose, the verifier is told the goal), which the authors say stops a stage from "confirming its own expectation".

## Limits / credibility
- Unreviewed v1 preprint; credibility-gate label: `context_or_prior_art_only`.
- Evaluation is entirely model-judged from frames (Qwen3.8-27B primary, GPT-5.5 on a 40-task subset, Claude Opus 5 for an audit on 40 games); no human rating of games except two example directors and the hidden-identity pairwise judges; rubric limited to four dimensions.
- The same model family acts as generator, play agent and judge in some rows; the strongest claim ("Qwen exceeds GPT-5.5 one-shot") depends on SFT from GPT-5.5 traces and on extra development rounds.
- Phaser comparisons against Play2Code are not all significant (Qwen: p = 0.394).
- Replay noise is large for some families (sports median range 4.66 points), so small differences within a family are unreliable.
- Heavy reliance on in-house baselines and on tasks that RSIGame's authors also use to tune thresholds (K, margin, checkpoint interval); the saturation setting K = 3 was chosen from the same runs ("on the same runs", App. C.2).
- Large parts of the artifact release are pending ("will be released upon approval").
